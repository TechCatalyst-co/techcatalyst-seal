What the seal attests

Of this business's last 10 reviews, at least 100% are verified by TechCatalyst, and the certificate is valid until its expiry date.

The window, the bar and the minimum review count are set per certificate. The ratio is re-evaluated continuously: if new unverified reviews push it below the bar, the seal turns amber on its own, before the certificate expires.

For businesses

Earn a seal from the reviews you already collect, then keep it by collecting them honestly. Every status change reaches every embedded seal within 30 seconds.

  1. Register your business

    We confirm you own your domain by DNS TXT record or by email to the domain contact. That website is the only one your seals can be bound to.

  2. Collect verified reviews

    Report real orders from the portal or the API and we send each customer a single-use review link. You can also import existing reviews from Google, Yelp, Facebook or a CSV for an analyst to audit.

    Verified purchase Audited · Google

  3. Receive your certificate

    Once your recent reviews meet the bar, we issue a certificate for up to 397 days. Renew it before it expires.

  4. Embed the seal

    Paste one line where the seal should appear. It renders in an isolated shadow root and refreshes itself.

    <script src="https://seal.techcatalyst.example/embed.js"
  data-tc-seal="seal_…" async></script>

Every review is screened before it counts

Each review is fraud-scored first. High-risk reviews are held for an analyst and never approved automatically. The signals are never shown to the business, so they can't be used to tune fake reviews.

  • The same IP address within 30 days
  • Bursts of reviews in a short window
  • Near-duplicate or copy-paste text
  • Disposable email domains
  • Embedded links
  • Very short, low-effort five-star text
  • Submissions seconds after the invite

Checking a seal

A picture of a seal proves nothing. Clicking it does.

  1. Click the seal. You'll land on our verification page, on our domain.
  2. Read the status and expiry. Only a green "Reviews Verified" with a future date means the certificate is in force.
  3. Compare the website. If the address bar you came from doesn't match the domain on that page, the seal was copied.
